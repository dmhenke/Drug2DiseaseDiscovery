Can a drug's structure predict its effect?

Stage P pilot on OP3, 140 compounds in human PBMCs · run Wed Nov 4 04:35:05 PM CST 2026 on qrtz-Precision-7820-Tower · commit 20a5847

No, not from a fingerprint. A ridge model on 2048-bit Morgan fingerprints does not predict a held-out compound's effect any better than simply using the average effect across all compounds in that cell type: a difference of 0.001 (−0.002 to 0.005) in correlation. Stage P fails, on both target measures.

The data is not at fault. A compound's measured effect in T and NK cells predicts its own effect in B and myeloid cells far better than the model does (0.269 against 0.189), so response predicts response. What fails is the link from structure to response.

Looking up the most chemically similar compound OP3 already measured beats the model on compound-specific signal, by 0.047 (0.018 to 0.077). The baseline built to be beaten is the method that works.

The one criterion that passed was badly specified, and I wrote it. known Ridge chose heavy regularisation (alpha 1000.0, 10000.0) and so sits almost exactly on the cell-type mean. Raw correlation rewards that, which means the cell-type mean on its own would also have “beaten” the nearest-compound baseline. It is not evidence that the model learned any chemistry.

The criterion is left as written rather than quietly rewritten. The column below that it should have used is the second one.

Raw correlation hides the thing we care about

Most of any correlation here comes from the cell type, not the compound: all compounds in myeloid cells look broadly alike. Removing the cell-type mean first leaves only the compound-specific part, which is what a drug-discovery claim rests on.

The dataset, and why the published split is not a test of new chemistry

OP3's own split holds out B and myeloid cells, not compounds: all 76 test compounds also appear in training in T and NK cells. It measures transfer between cell types. That is why the verdict above uses a whole-compound holdout instead, and why the published split can be used to show that compound signal is present.

Both target measures agree

The run was repeated against clipped signed log10 p, the measure the published benchmark scores on. logFC decides the verdict here because p-value-based measures scale with how many cells a cell type contributed, while an effect size does not.

What this changes

  • Do not scale the same model. The rule set before the run was that a bigger model of the same kind does not follow a failure like this. known
  • Test the molecule representation next, holding everything else fixed. Same folds, same seed, same baselines, with a pretrained chemical language model in place of the fingerprint. If that also cannot beat nearest compound, the limit is 140 compounds of sparse chemistry rather than the encoder. inferred
  • Denser chemistry matters more than a bigger model. Pretraining on LINCS L1000, roughly 30 000 compounds, is the only route to enough chemical coverage for a structure-to-response map. This result is evidence for that, not against it. inferred
  • For the lupus application today, use the lookup. For any candidate within about 0.4 of something OP3 measured, the defensible answer is that neighbour's measured profile. 15 of our own candidate compounds were measured in OP3 directly, so for those there is no prediction to make. known

Honest limits

  • One seed, one set of folds. Seed 0; stability across seeds was not tested.
  • No noise ceiling. These summarised tables carry no replicates, so how much of the unexplained variation is simply measurement noise cannot be estimated from them.
  • The similarity bins were chosen after seeing results and the intervals are not adjusted for having looked. Treat that section as a lead.
  • Healthy cells, in culture. OP3 is PBMCs from 3 healthy donors at a single dose for 24 hours. Nothing here tests prediction in diseased cells, which is where this pipeline is aimed.
  • A linear model on a binary fingerprint is a floor, not a fair test of chemistry. It cannot represent a compound acting differently in different cell types. That is deliberate for a baseline, and it is why the next step is the representation.

Reproducing this

On the compute node, one command runs the content checks, then the pilot, and packs the outputs:

scripts/op3_report.sh

Inputs are the Open Problems build of OP3 (de_train.h5ad, de_test.h5ad), fetched by scripts/fetch_op3.sh. 5 folds, 20 permutations for the null, 2000 bootstrap resamples, seed 0. The model, baselines and criteria are in scripts/op3_ridge.py, with a self-test in scripts/test_op3_ridge.py that requires a planted signal to be recovered and pure noise not to be. The outputs this page is built from are in analysis/op3_stage_p/, and every number here is read or recomputed from them by scripts/build_op3_page.py, which fails if a recomputed value disagrees with the run's own summary.
